# Networking Follow-Up for Mark Neace

Mark attends 4+ networking events every week. He meets a lot of people. This skill turns his messy post-event notes into ready-to-send follow-up emails and a clean contact log. Follow-ups go out within 24-48 hours of the event, so speed and low friction matter more than perfection.

## The workflow

1. **Take in whatever Mark gives you.** Rough notes, bullet fragments, photos of business cards, transcribed voice memos. Don't ask him to reformat anything. Pull out: name, company, role, which event, what they talked about, and anything personal he noted (kids, golf, causes).
2. **Classify each contact** into one of the four types below. If a contact fits two types, pick the one with the nearest money or mission, and mention the second angle inside the email.
3. **Draft one follow-up email per contact** in Mark's voice (read `references/voice-profile.md` before writing any email).
4. **Output the contact log rows** so Mark can paste them into his tracker sheet.
5. **Never send anything.** Every email is a draft for Mark to review. If Gmail tools are connected, create drafts in Gmail; otherwise present the drafts as text. This is a hard rule — Mark reviews everything before it reaches a human.

## Contact types and next steps

| Type | Who they are | Default next step in the email |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Business owner or leader who could buy from Mark | Invite to the Story Self Check ($49) or offer a coffee/discovery chat. Pick ONE, not both. |
| Referral partner | Fractional c-suite, M&A firm, business coach, banker, attorney, HR exec, marketing/PR/web agency | Suggest coffee or a short call to learn how to send business their way |
| Podcast / speaking lead | Podcast host, event organizer, association leader | Offer Mark as a guest; mention one talk topic that fits their audience |
| Community / service | Nonprofit, Rotary, cause connection | Offer help or a connection first; no sales ask at all |

Mark's talk topics (for podcast/speaking leads): "Purpose Driven Content Strategy," "Brand Story That Builds Trust & Value," "Communicating Servant Leadership."

## Email rules

- Under 120 words. Busy people read short emails.
- Open with the specific thing they talked about, never "great to meet you" alone.
- One ask per email. Two asks means neither happens.
- Serve-first framing: lead with something useful to THEM (an intro, a resource, an invite to one of his groups) before any ask, whenever the notes make that possible.
- Sign-off is always some form of "let me know how I can serve you" followed by "Mark."
- Subject lines are plain and human: "Good meeting you at Netweavers" beats anything clever.
- If the notes mention something personal, use it in one natural sentence. Don't force it.
- If Mark's notes are too thin to personalize (just a name and company), write the email anyway using the event as the shared context, and flag it to Mark as "thin notes, consider adding a detail."

## Contact log format

Output one row per contact, ready for a Google Sheet with these columns:

`Name | Company | Role | Type | Event | Date | What we discussed | Next step | Status`

Status starts as "Draft ready." Dates in MM/DD/YYYY.
